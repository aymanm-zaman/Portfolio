Mechanical Design Sep – Dec 2024

4×2 Compact CNC Router

Led a four-member team through the full product design and development process for a compact desktop CNC router, delivering a machine approximately 35% less costly than comparable market alternatives.

~35% Cost reduction vs. market
4×2 ft Work envelope
4 Team members
Role Team Leader
Course Mechanical Engineering Design
Duration Sep – Dec 2024
Tools SolidWorks, Excel, McMaster-Carr

Overview

The objective was to design a compact desktop CNC router capable of precision milling while significantly undercutting the cost of commercially available alternatives. Most comparable 4′×4′ machines on the market were substantially more expensive and oversized for small-scale manufacturing and prototyping use cases. Our 4′×2′ design targeted the same capability at a fraction of the price.

Design Approach

I led the team through concept generation, down-selection, and detailed design. We integrated custom-machined components with off-the-shelf McMaster-Carr hardware to balance cost, lead time, and performance.

GD&T and DFM/DFA principles were applied throughout to define fits, tolerances, and surface finish requirements — ensuring parts could be fabricated and assembled without ambiguity. The CAD assembly was fully parametric in SolidWorks, allowing rapid iteration as design constraints evolved.

Validation & Deliverables

System validation results and performance trade-off analyses were presented to the Engineering Manager, supported by market benchmarking data, engineering calculations, and SolidWorks simulations.

Final deliverables included:

  • Fully toleranced parametric CAD assembly
  • 2D fabrication drawings for all custom components
  • Bill of Materials (BOM) for procurement and cost tracking
  • Engineering presentation with validation results and trade-off analysis
