Redesigned the rear wing mounting structure for the UT25 Formula SAE vehicle, achieving a 12% weight reduction through FEA-driven optimisation while maintaining structural integrity under aerodynamic loading.

Overview

The UT25 Formula SAE vehicle required a redesigned rear wing mounting structure capable of supporting aerodynamic loads under high-speed cornering while minimising weight — a critical constraint in Formula SAE competition. The previous design had excess material that contributed unnecessary mass without proportional structural benefit.

Analysis

Structural analysis was performed in ANSYS FEA to evaluate the mounting structure under the aerodynamic load cases derived from CFD simulations of the rear wing. The analysis covered:

  • Structural stiffness under peak downforce loading
  • Vibration response and natural frequency assessment
  • Load path through the mounting interface to the monocoque

Topology Optimisation was then applied to identify and remove low-stress material regions, directly targeting the 12% weight reduction without compromising stiffness or factor of safety.

Fabrication

I contributed directly to manufacturing of the final components, accumulating over 30 hours in the machine shop. Work included machining brackets and mounting hardware on lathes and mills, as well as fabrication of accumulator walls using saws and sheet metal processes.

Manufacturability, process flow, and material selection were considered throughout the design phase to ensure the final geometry could be produced efficiently within team resources.
