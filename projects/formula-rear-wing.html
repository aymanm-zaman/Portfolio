UT Formula SAE — Rear Wing Mounts
Role Team Member, Aerodynamics
Team UT Formula Racing
Duration Aug 2024 to Present
Tools ANSYS FEA, SolidWorks, Lathes & Mills

Overview

The UT25 Formula SAE vehicle's previous rear wing mounting structure carried excess material that added weight without proportional structural benefit. The goal was to redesign the mounts to support the full aerodynamic load envelope, including peak downforce under high-speed cornering, while reducing mass, a critical constraint in Formula SAE competition.

Reflections

This was my first time using ANSYS, and my first time seeing FEA applied to something real. Up to that point, stress and strain were things I'd solved on paper in class. Watching those same concepts come alive as colour maps on a part I'd helped design was the moment mechanical engineering clicked for me in a way it hadn't before.

The work I did was focused and specific. I wasn't redesigning the full aero package. But knowing that the part I analysed and helped machine ended up on the final car made it feel significant. I also spent a lot of time in the shop fabricating components for the team, and found that I genuinely enjoyed machining. That experience led directly to my summer job as a Machine Shop Assistant.

Analysis

Structural analysis was performed in ANSYS to evaluate the mounting structure under aerodynamic load cases derived from CFD simulations of the rear wing. The analysis covered structural stiffness under peak downforce, vibration response and natural frequency assessment, and load path through the mounting interface to the chassis.

Topology optimisation was then used to identify and remove low-stress material regions, targeting the 12% weight reduction without compromising stiffness or factor of safety.

Fabrication

I supported manufacturing directly, spending over 30 hours in the machine shop. Work included machining brackets, mounts, and accumulator walls on lathes, mills, and saws. Manufacturability and material selection were considered throughout the design phase to ensure the final geometry could be produced within the team's resources.
